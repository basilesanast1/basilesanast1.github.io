Summary

Greece has officially passed a new anti-piracy law aimed at cracking down on unauthorized distribution of digital content. The legislation, which came into effect in early April 2025, introduces stricter penalties and faster response times for removing pirated material from the internet.

Why Is That

Many Greek households and watching illegally sports events which are normally hosted by Cable TV providers. The subscriptions here are really high (about 20€/month) and the average salary being just 850€/moth makes Cable TV simply unaffordable. So Greek residents are turning to cheaper of even free options. Streaming Cable TV channels illegally, buying illegal IPTV Set-Top-Boxes and so on. Greek businesses are also starting to adapt to this trend. Personally, I have seen a lot of coffee shops, taverns and even bakeries that are using an illegal IPTV Box to stream TV channels (those 10€ chinese boxes) that have Kodi, Stremio and all sorts of piracy apps. Personally owning one of these boxes allows me to say that they are very unsafe in terms of privacy. Considering that more than 70% of the illegal IPTV connections are in HTTP, makes it easy for the Greek government to find them and fine you, the consumer.

Why Is The Greek Government Fining Consumers?

Fines that go as high as 750€ are one of the ways the government is using to scare people off of piracy. Government officials claim to have found a way to link your IP address to the one who is paying the Internet bill, and send the fine there. This requires a special order signed by a prosecutor asking the Greek ISPs to reveal the subscriber's name, address and billing info.
